Harbour market reopens after two years of repairs

By A. Reporter, 4 November 2026

The old harbour market opened its gates again on Wednesday morning, two years after a winter storm tore away most of its roof. By seven o'clock a queue had formed along the quay, and the first loaves were sold before the ribbon had been cut. This is a sample article, written for tests and simulation, and nothing in it happened.

Sixty stalls have returned, a few more than the market held before it closed. Most of the old names are back: the two fishmongers by the east door, the spice seller who has traded here for thirty years, and the family that sells nothing but eggs. They are joined by a dozen newcomers, among them a coffee roaster, a repair bench for bicycles and a stall that lends tools by the day.

The repairs cost a little under four million and were paid for by the town, a heritage grant and a collection among the traders themselves. The new roof is glass and timber, and it lets in far more light than the iron one it replaces. Rainwater from it now runs into two tanks under the floor, which the market uses to wash down the fish counters every evening.

"We lost two winters of trade, and some of us nearly lost the business," said one stallholder, who asked not to be named in a sample article. "But people kept asking when we would be back. That is the only reason any of us are standing here this morning."

Not everything is finished. The west entrance is still boarded up, the car park behind it will not open until spring, and the upper gallery, which is meant to hold a small museum of the harbour, is an empty room with a ladder in it. The market's manager says the gallery will open "when it is ready, and not a day before".

For the town, the reopening is about more than shopping. The market was the reason many visitors walked down to the water at all, and the cafes along the quay say their takings fell by a third while it was shut. Three of them have taken on staff again this month.

The market is open from Tuesday to Sunday, from seven in the morning until two in the afternoon. On the first Saturday of each month it will stay open into the evening, with the fish counters closed and the lamps lit, for what the traders are calling the long market.
